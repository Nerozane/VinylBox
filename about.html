About

The website

I built VinylBox to catalogue my own vinyl. I DJ house, disco, and techno and my collection is too big to keep in my head—I needed a shelf I could actually browse.

Discogs is useful for storing what you own, but when I plan a set I want BPM and key on every track, in one place. Discogs does not show that the way I need. VinylBox lets me log records with tracklists and optional BPM per song so I can prep before a gig. Key is something I still want to add here for the same reason.

This site is my Website Development 2 college project—I used HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Handlebars for templates, and Swiper for the carousels to build the site.

The collection

Each record holds the usual stuff — title, artist, year, genre, and label. Where I have them, I add front and back sleeve photos too. Under that goes the tracklist, and I can log a BPM on individual tracks when I know it, which helps when I'm planning a set.

The shelf saves automatically in your browser, so your collection is still there next time you open the site. Nothing gets sent anywhere — it all stays on your machine.

Who built it

Nathan Reilly. I go by Just Nathan as my DJ name.

I'm a Computing student at SETU. Outside college I spend most of my time digging for records and playing out — my sound sits between deep house, disco edits, and the dustier end of techno. Anything with groove and a bit of soul.

Vinyl is the format I keep coming back to. There's something about handling a record, reading the back of the sleeve, and dropping the needle that streaming will never match. The trouble is keeping track of what you own once the crate gets heavy — that's where VinylBox came from. I wanted my own shelf, organised the way I think about records when I'm planning a set: tracklist, genre, label, and BPM where I know it.

Long term I'd like to add key detection, harmonic mixing notes, and a way to build provisional setlists straight from the catalog. For now this site is the foundation.
